## 2

Question: How can I tell if my product is a medical device?

Outcome: answered

Answer: To determine whether a product qualifies as a medical device, confirm that it meets the definition of a medical device set out in Article 2(1) of the MDR in accordance with its intended purpose and principal mode of action. A medical device means any instrument, apparatus, appliance, software, implant, reagent, material or other article intended by the manufacturer to be used, alone or in combination, for human beings for one or more specific medical purposes (such as diagnosis, prevention, monitoring, prediction, prognosis, treatment, or alleviation of disease; diagnosis, monitoring, treatment, alleviation of, or compensation for, an injury or disability; investigation, replacement or modification of the anatomy or of a physiological or pathological process or state; providing information by means of in vitro examination of specimens derived from the human body; devices for the control or support of conception; or products specifically intended for cleaning, disinfection, or sterilisation of devices) and which does not achieve its principal intended action by pharmacological, immunological or metabolic means in or on the human body, though it may be assisted in its function by such means. If a manufacturer assigns several different intended purposes to a product, not all of which fall under the MDR, it qualifies as a medical device only with respect to those intended medical purposes covered by Article 2(1). For borderline products where this determination is difficult, consult primarily the information available on the European Commission website or seek guidance from your competent authority.

Question: How are medical devices classified under the MDR?

Outcome: answered

Answer: Under MDR Annex VIII, there are two classification rules applying specifically to substance-based devices that take into account relevant risks: Rule 3 and Rule 21. Not all substance-based devices fall under these two rules, in which case they are classified under the general rules.

## 4

Question: What does a manufacturer need to do before selling a medical device in the EU?

Outcome: answered

Answer: Before placing a device on the market, the manufacturer will affix the CE mark in accordance with Annex V and draw up the EU declaration of conformity, including all the information required by Annex IV. Prior to that, the manufacturer will demonstrate conformity with the MDR and compliance with the applicable general safety and performance requirements laid out in Annex I. Additionally, the manufacturer will carry out tasks such as putting in place a quality management system and a system for risk management according to Article 10(2) and 10(9), conducting a clinical evaluation, conducting a conformity assessment, drawing up and keeping up-to-date technical documentation, drawing up an EU declaration of conformity, and submitting required information to the electronic system for registration of economic operators (Eudamed) and registering the device.

Question: When does a medical device manufacturer need a notified body?

Outcome: answered

Answer: In the case of Class I medical devices, the intervention of a notified body is required when devices are placed on the market in sterile condition, have a measuring function, or are reusable surgical instruments. In all other cases, the intervention of a notified body is not required for Class I devices.

Question: What should I include in a medical device’s technical documentation?

Outcome: answered

Answer: The technical documentation must be prepared according to Annex II and III of the MDR and cover all relevant aspects, such as: the rationale for the qualification as a medical device and the risk class attributed; a general description of the device including its intended purpose, intended users/patient population, accessories, variants, and the Basic UDI-DI; technical specifications including raw materials, drawings of components, and quality control procedures; information to be supplied by the manufacturer (labels, packaging, and instructions for use); an overview of previous generations of the device and similar devices; design and manufacturing information including the identification of all sites, suppliers, and sub-contractors; and information demonstrating conformity with the general safety and performance requirements set out in Annex I (typically presented as a checklist specifying applicability, solutions adopted, applied standards or common specifications, and where to find evidence).

## 8

Question: What is a clinical evaluation, and why is it needed?

Outcome: answered

Answer: Manufacturers shall plan, continuously conduct, and document a clinical evaluation. The clinical evaluation shall be thorough and objective, and take into account both favourable and unfavourable data. Its depth and extent shall be proportionate and appropriate to the nature, classification, intended purpose and risks of the device in question, as well as to the manufacturer's claims in respect of the device. The results of the clinical evaluation and the clinical evidence on which it is based shall be documented in a clinical evaluation report which shall support the assessment of the conformity of the device. The clinical evidence together with non-clinical data generated from non-clinical testing methods and other relevant documentation shall allow the manufacturer to demonstrate conformity with the general safety and performance requirements and shall be part of the technical documentation for the device in question.

Question: What information should come with a medical device?

Outcome: answered

Answer: Each device must be accompanied by any safety and performance information needed to use it safely and to identify the device as well as the manufacturer and/or the authorised representative, taking account of the training and knowledge of the potential users. This information comprises the label, device packaging and the data in the instructions for use. By way of derogation to the general principles, no instructions for use are required for Class I devices if they can be used properly and safely without such instruction, with an exception most likely posed for Class Ir devices as reprocessing will require an instruction.

## 15

Question: What is a UDI, and why is it used?
