Question: How can I tell if my product is a medical device?

Outcome: answered

Answer: To determine whether a product qualifies as a medical device, you must confirm that it meets the definition of a 'medical device' set out in Article 2(1) of the MDR in accordance with its intended purpose and principal mode of action. Under Article 2(1), a medical device is any instrument, apparatus, appliance, software, implant, reagent, material or other article intended by the manufacturer to be used, alone or in combination, for human beings for one or more specific medical purposes (such as diagnosis, prevention, monitoring, prediction, prognosis, treatment, or alleviation of disease; investigation, replacement, or modification of the anatomy or of a physiological or pathological process; control or support of conception; or products for the cleaning, disinfection, or sterilisation of devices) and which does not achieve its principal intended action by pharmacological, immunological, or metabolic means in or on the human body, though it may be assisted in its function by such means.

Question: How are medical devices classified under the MDR?

Outcome: answered

Answer: Under the MDR, devices are divided into classes I, IIa, IIb, and III, taking into account their intended purpose and inherent risks. Classification is carried out in accordance with Annex VIII.

## 4

Question: What does a manufacturer need to do before selling a medical device in the EU?

Outcome: answered

Answer: Before placing a device on the market, the manufacturer will affix the CE mark in accordance with Annex V and draw up the EU declaration of conformity, including all the information required by Annex IV. Prior to that, the manufacturer will demonstrate conformity with the MDR and compliance with the applicable general safety and performance requirements laid out in Annex I.

Citation [2]: Guidance notes for manufacturers of class I medical devices; document=mdcg-2019-15; version=Original - December 2019; source=mdcg-2019-15@sha256:6c32134a74411cba3fc2545e75485b42e2d590f89a976bcf431f5e901918c7a2; URL=https://ec.europa.eu/docsroom/documents/34922; pages=5; locators=none; hierarchies=Introduction; permitted regimes=MDR

Answer: To accomplish these tasks, the manufacturer must carry out the following: put in place a quality management system and a system for risk management according to Article 10(2) and 10(9); conduct a clinical evaluation in accordance with Article 61 as established in Article 10(3) and Annex XV; conduct a conformity assessment according to Article 52(7) (requesting the involvement of a Notified Body in specific cases such as sterile devices, devices with measuring function, or reusable surgical instruments); draw up and keep up-to-date technical documentation related to devices as set out in Annexes II and III in accordance with Article 10(4); draw up an EU declaration of conformity in accordance with Article 19; submit required information to Eudamed and comply with registration obligations, using the Single Registration Number (SRN) when applying to a Notified Body or accessing Eudamed; and register the device in Eudamed by assigning the Basic UDI-DI, providing core data elements to the UDI database, and assigning a UDI to the device and higher levels of packaging for identification and traceability.

Question: When does a medical device manufacturer need a notified body?

Outcome: answered

Answer: Prior to placing a device on the market or putting a device into service that is not placed on the market, manufacturers must undertake an assessment of conformity. For class I devices, the involvement of a notified body is not required in all cases, but manufacturers of class I devices placed on the market in sterile condition, having a measuring function, or being reusable surgical instruments must apply specific conformity assessment procedures requiring the involvement of a notified body.

Outcome: answered

## 7

Question: What should I include in a medical device’s technical documentation?

Outcome: answered

Answer: The manufacturer will draw up and keep up to date technical documentation demonstrating conformity with the technical requirements of the MDR, prepared according to Annex II and III prior to drawing up the EU declaration of conformity. It must be presented in a clear, organised, readily searchable, and unambiguous manner, and be made available to competent authorities, authorised representatives, and notified bodies as applicable. The technical documentation covers relevant aspects from Annex II and III, including: the rationale for medical device qualification and risk class attributed; a general description of the device (intended purpose, users, patient population, accessories, variants, and Basic UDI-DI); technical specifications (raw materials, component drawings, master patterns, quality control procedures); information supplied by the manufacturer (labels, packaging, instructions for use in Member State languages); references to previous generations and similar devices; design and manufacturing information (design/manufacturing understanding, qualification tests, design calculations, identification of sites/suppliers/subcontractors); and information demonstrating conformity with the general safety and performance requirements set out in Annex I (typically presented as a checklist detailing applicability, adopted solutions, harmonized standards or common specifications applied, and evidence locations).

Citation [1]: Guidance notes for manufacturers of class I medical devices; document=mdcg-2019-15; version=Original - December 2019; source=mdcg-2019-15@sha256:6c32134a74411cba3fc2545e75485b42e2d590f89a976bcf431f5e901918c7a2; URL=https://ec.europa.eu/docsroom/documents/34922; pages=14, 15; locators=none; hierarchies=Placing Class I medical devices on the market: > 2) Confirm product as a Class I medical device > c) Prepare technical documentation; permitted regimes=MDR

Answer: Additionally, the technical documentation must list relevant harmonised standards applied in full or in part, incorporate benefit-risk analysis and risk management information, include preclinical and clinical evaluation data results, and contain the post-market surveillance system documentation (addressing Annex III point 1.1 elements, including a post-market clinical follow-up plan or justification, and the Article 85 PMS report). Manufacturers must keep the technical documentation available for competent authorities for at least 10 years after the last device covered by the EU declaration of conformity has been placed on the market, and provide it in an official Union language determined by the concerned Member State upon request.

Citation [4]: Guidance notes for manufacturers of class I medical devices; document=mdcg-2019-15; version=Original - December 2019; source=mdcg-2019-15@sha256:6c32134a74411cba3fc2545e75485b42e2d590f89a976bcf431f5e901918c7a2; URL=https://ec.europa.eu/docsroom/documents/34922; pages=15, 16; locators=none; hierarchies=Placing Class I medical devices on the market: > 2) Confirm product as a Class I medical device > c) Prepare technical documentation; permitted regimes=MDR

Answer: For devices generally, the technical documentation must also include the risk class of the device and the justification for the classification rule(s) applied in accordance with Annex VIII of the MDR.

## 8

Question: What is a clinical evaluation, and why is it needed?

Outcome: answered

Answer: Manufacturers must plan, continuously conduct, and document a clinical evaluation. The clinical evaluation shall be thorough and objective, taking into account both favourable and unfavourable data, and its depth and extent shall be proportionate and appropriate to the nature, classification, intended purpose, and risks of the device in question, as well as to the manufacturer's claims.

## 17

Outcome: answered

Answer: Manufacturers of devices made available on the Union market (other than investigational devices) must report any serious incident involving their devices (except for expected side-effects that are clearly documented, quantified, and subject to trend reporting) and any field safety corrective action. For serious incidents, manufacturers shall report immediately after establishing the causal relationship (or when it is reasonably possible) and not later than 15 days after becoming aware of the incident. In the event of a serious public health threat, the report must be provided immediately and not later than 2 days after becoming aware of the threat. In the event of death or an unanticipated serious deterioration in a person's state of health, the report must be provided immediately after establishing or suspecting a causal relationship (or as soon as it is suspected) and not later than 10 days after becoming aware of the serious incident. If uncertain whether an incident is reportable after becoming aware of it, the manufacturer must still submit a report within the required timeframe.
