Question: How can I tell if my product is a medical device?

Outcome: answered

Answer: To determine whether a product qualifies as a medical device, you must confirm that it meets the definition of a 'medical device' set out in Article 2(1) of the MDR in accordance with its intended purpose and principal mode of action. Specifically, it must be an instrument, apparatus, appliance, software, implant, reagent, material, or other article intended by the manufacturer to be used, alone or in combination, for human beings for one or more specific medical purposes (such as diagnosis, prevention, monitoring, prediction, prognosis, treatment, or alleviation of disease, injury, or disability; investigation, replacement, or modification of the anatomy or of a physiological process; control or support of conception; or cleaning, disinfection, or sterilisation of devices), and it must not achieve its principal intended action by pharmacological, immunological, or metabolic means in or on the human body, though it may be assisted by such means. If a manufacturer assigns several different intended purposes to a product, not all of which fall under the MDR, it qualifies as a medical device only with respect to the intended medical purposes covered by Article 2(1). Accessories to medical devices, while not medical devices per se, are covered by MDR provisions and fall under the term 'device' in the meaning of the MDR (with the exception of accessories to devices covered by the MDR solely by virtue of Annex XVI). For borderline products, you should consult information available on the European Commission website or your competent authority.

Outcome: answered

Answer: Devices are divided into classes I, IIa, IIb, and III, taking into account their intended purpose and inherent risks. Classification must be carried out in accordance with Annex VIII of Regulation (EU) 2017/745.

## 7

Question: What should I include in a medical device’s technical documentation?

Outcome: answered

Answer: The manufacturer must draw up and keep up to date technical documentation prepared according to Annex II and III of the MDR, prior to drawing up the EU declaration of conformity. It must be presented in a clear, organized, readily searchable, and unambiguous manner, and include the risk class of the device and the justification for the classification rule(s) applied in accordance with Annex VIII. The documentation must cover all relevant aspects from Annex II and III, including: rationale for qualification as a medical device and risk class; a general description of the device (including intended purpose, users/patient population, accessories, variants, and Basic UDI-DI); technical specifications (raw materials, component drawings, master patterns, quality control procedures); information to be supplied by the manufacturer (labels and instructions for use in the languages determined by the Member States where sold); references to previous generations and similar devices; design and manufacturing information (including test results, design calculations, and identification of design/manufacturing sites and suppliers); general safety and performance requirements information demonstrating conformity with Annex I (including harmonized standards and/or common specifications in the form of a checklist); benefit-risk analysis and risk management; pre-clinical and clinical evaluation data; and a post-market surveillance system and plan (including PMCF or justification for non-applicability and the PMS report). Furthermore, manufacturers must keep the documentation available for competent authorities for at least 10 years after the last device covered by the EU declaration of conformity has been placed on the market.

Question: What information should come with a medical device?

Outcome: answered

Answer: Each device must be accompanied by any safety and performance information needed to use it safely and to identify the device as well as the manufacturer and/or the authorised representative, taking account of the training and knowledge of the potential users. This information comprises the label, device packaging and the data in the instructions for use.

Question: What does the person responsible for regulatory compliance do?

Outcome: answered

Answer: The person responsible for regulatory compliance shall at least be responsible for ensuring that: (a) the conformity of the devices is appropriately checked, in accordance with the quality management system under which the devices are manufactured, before a device is released; (b) the technical documentation and the EU declaration of conformity are drawn up and kept up-to-date; (c) the post-market surveillance obligations are complied with in accordance with Article 10(10); (d) the reporting obligations referred to in Articles 87 to 91 are fulfilled; and (e) in the case of investigational devices, the statement referred to in Section 4.1 of Chapter II of Annex XV is issued.

Question: What should a manufacturer do after its device is on the market?

Outcome: answered

Answer: After placing the Class I device on the market, the manufacturer will follow the next Post Market Surveillance (PMS) steps.
